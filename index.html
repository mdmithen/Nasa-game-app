Junior Astronaut
Mission Trainer

Manage Your Moon Base. Protect Your Crew.

You are the Mission Commander of Base Selene, a small lunar outpost. First you choose where to land, then three astronauts depend on you for oxygen, water, food and electricity. Every day brings a new problem: a broken solar panel, a leaking tank, a dust storm. Your choices decide whether everyone comes home.

No install, no internet needed to play. Open index.html in any browser.

How to Play

The goal

You are the Commander walking around Base Selene. Keep all three astronauts healthy, complete all 6 mission objectives, and do it inside 16 mission days. If a vital resource or crew health reaches zero, the mission fails.

Moving around

A Dor ← →Walk left and right
W / Spaceor ↑Jump — reach floating supply canisters
EInteract with the station you are standing at
EscPause or resume

On a phone use the on-screen buttons. On a keyboard you can also press 1 2 3 to pick a decision option.

Every action costs a day

  1. Walk to a station marked with a glowing !
  2. Press E. A panel opens showing the situation.
  3. Pick an option. Each option really changes your resources.
  4. Read what your choice did, and why.
  5. Press Finish and End Day. Only then does the day tick over.

Time is a resource here. Walking around is free, but every station you actually use costs one of your sixteen days.

Stations

  • Habitat — crew health and rest.
  • Oxygen System — air supply and the CO2 scrubber.
  • Water Recycler — the loop that reuses water.
  • Food Station — hydroponics and food stores.
  • Solar Panels — electricity generation.
  • Supply Depot — spare parts, rations and the rover.
  • Mission Console — objectives and mission status.

The resources

  • Oxygen for breathing. It is never recycled in this simplified game.
  • Water for drinking, washing and growing food.
  • Food the crew eats every day.
  • Electricity made by solar panels and spent by everything else.
  • Crew Health drops when a vital resource runs low.

Important

The numbers in this game are simplified teaching values, not real NASA operational measurements. Real life support engineering is far more complex.

Learn About Space

Real science and game rules are two different things. Every card below is labelled. REAL SCIENCE cards come from published NASA and scientific sources. GAME RULE cards are invented for teaching and are not real measurements.

The Moon's environment

REAL SCIENCE

The Moon has almost no atmosphere — only a very thin exosphere, far too thin to breathe. It has no global magnetic field like Earth's. Because it does not keep an atmosphere, the temperature on the surface swings enormously between day and night, and its surface is constantly bombarded by radiation from the Sun and from space.

Source: NASA Science — Moon Facts; NASA lunar science mission pages.

Why astronauts need oxygen

REAL SCIENCE

Humans need oxygen to turn food into energy and cannot survive without it for more than a few minutes. On the Moon there is nothing to breathe, so a habitat must bring or make its own air. Inside a pressurised habitat astronauts wear normal clothing and breathe a mixture similar to Earth's air; only outside, in a spacesuit, do they carry oxygen tanks.

Source: NASA — spacesuit and life-support documentation.

Water is precious and reusable

REAL SCIENCE

A lunar base cannot ship every drop of water it needs, so real designs recover it. Water vapour in the air is condensed, urine and sweat are filtered and cleaned, and grey water is reused for washing and for plants. In 2008 NASA's LCROSS mission detected water ice at the lunar south pole, and later instruments found ice in permanently shadowed craters — a possible resource for a future base.

Source: NASA LCROSS mission results; NASA lunar exploration research.

Solar energy and the long lunar night

REAL SCIENCE

Apollo spacecraft and instruments on the Moon have used solar panels successfully. The catch is the lunar day and night cycle: one full cycle of sunlight and darkness takes about 29.5 Earth days, so any location spends roughly half of that time in darkness. That is exactly why a real lunar base needs batteries, and why a damaged solar panel is such a serious problem.

Source: NASA lunar science and mission pages.

Growing food off Earth

REAL SCIENCE

NASA has already grown plants inside space stations. The Advanced Plant Habitat, nicknamed "Veggie", has been part of the International Space Station since 2016 and has grown edible crops such as lettuce under artificial light. Moon or Mars bases would need hydroponics, grow lights and a controlled water system for the same reason.

Source: NASA — Advanced Plant Habitat / Veggie, International Space Station.

What makes lunar living hard

REAL SCIENCE

Lunar dust is sharp, clingy and electrostatically charged, so it gets into seals and scratches equipment. Gravity is about one sixth of Earth's, which changes how you move and how fluids behave. Getting supplies from Earth takes days, and Earth radio is far away — light takes about 1.3 seconds to travel there, so messages must wait.

Source: NASA lunar science and human exploration documentation.

About this game's numbers

GAME RULE

Every bar in this game — oxygen, water, food, electricity, health — is a simplified teaching value chosen so the arithmetic stays easy to follow on paper. They are not NASA measurements, not flight-planning figures, and not predictions of what a real mission would look like. A real base has far more systems and far more ways to fail.
